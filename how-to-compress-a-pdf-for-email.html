Compression Tutorial

How to Compress a PDF for Email

Most email and portal limits are not asking for the smallest possible file. They are asking for a file small enough to send while still remaining readable. That is why the best compression setting is often not the most aggressive one.

Recommended Workflow

  1. Open the Compress PDF tool.
  2. Start with the balanced compression level.
  3. Check the output size and zoom in on small text or signatures.
  4. Only move to aggressive compression if the file is still too large.
  5. Use image-focused compression when the PDF is mostly scans or photos.

What Usually Shrinks Best

Large scanned pages, embedded images, thumbnails, and unused font data often create the biggest savings. Text-first PDFs with clean vector content may already be fairly compact.

What to Review After Compression

Review signatures, form fields, charts, and small labels. These are the areas where over-compression becomes noticeable fastest.

Good Targets for Common Use Cases

  • Email attachment: aim to stay comfortably below the mail limit.
  • Job application portal: verify the exact per-file maximum listed by the form.
  • Website upload: keep a margin for retries and future edits.
  • Mobile sharing: prefer readability over chasing the smallest possible file.
