JPG files are fine for quick sharing, but they become awkward when you need something that feels like a proper document. Printing, page order, and upload consistency are usually easier once the images live inside a PDF.

## When JPG to PDF is the right move

This workflow is useful for:

- scanned forms
- receipts photographed on a phone
- signed pages captured as images
- photos that need to print like a document

If several images belong together, PDF is usually the cleaner container.

## Step 1: Put images in the right order first

Open [JPG to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf) or [Image to PDF](/image-to-pdf) only after you know the right page order. This matters more than people expect, especially when the images came from a phone gallery with inconsistent names.

## Step 2: Check orientation before export

Rotate sideways pages before final export if possible. Printing problems often come from image orientation rather than the PDF container itself.

## Step 3: Export one clean PDF

Once the order looks correct, export the images as a single PDF. This makes the file easier to:

- print
- email
- archive
- submit to portals

## Step 4: Compress later if the PDF is too large

Phone photos can create a bigger PDF than you expect. If the final file is too large for email or upload, use [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) after the conversion.

## Step 5: Organize pages if you mixed sources

If the final PDF combines photos from different places or different days, [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf) can help you review sequence and remove mistakes after conversion.

## Common mistakes

### Sending loose images instead of one document

That makes printing and record-keeping harder.

### Keeping the wrong order

Page order is one of the first things reviewers notice.

### Forgetting size limits

High-resolution phone photos can create surprisingly large PDFs.

If the next step is sharing visual previews rather than printing, you may also need the reverse workflow: PDF to JPG.
